About SailKind

SailKind Global Voices is a world map of ocean conservation stories, told by the people who live and work beside the water. Founded by Makenna Earl, it gathers first-hand accounts of what is changing along coasts around the world, what communities are doing about it, and what they wish everyone understood.

Anyone can add their community's story with the Share your story form. Each one is reviewed before it appears on the map.

Tamatoa · Lyon

Lyon, French Polynesia · Plastic & cleanups
Shared by Tamatoa · 2026-10

About me

I work as a Management Controller for a logistics company. I enjoy fishing or simply relaxing by the beach.

Our community and the ocean

The ocean is for my community a source of revenue, food and entertainment. It is central to our lives. In the marquesas islands, pollution and overfishing hasn’t gone out of control yet and we can still have access to an abundance of fishes to provide for our families.

An issue we see

The rising of pollution in the lagoons. The lack of appropriate waste management infrastructure is impacting the earth and with the rain the lagoons directly.

What we're doing

Growing up in the Marquesas Islands, I learned that the ocean is not just a resource but part of our identity, culture, and way of life. We can protect it by strengthening traditional Polynesian knowledge, educating younger generations, and supporting locally managed marine areas that allow fish populations and marine ecosystems to recover.

What I wish the world understood

I wish people understood that in Polynesia, the ocean is not a barrier separating islands but a source of life that connects our people, our ancestors, and our future. Conservation efforts should listen to local communities and respect our ancestral knowledge, because the people who live closest to the ocean often have some of the deepest connections to protecting it.

Biggest issues here

Plastic pollution, Decline of marine wildlife & biodiversity, Coral reef damage

Tanya · Douglas

Douglas, Isle of Man · Plastic & cleanups
Shared by Tanya · 2026-10

About me

I live on the Isle of Man and work as a Maritime Security Supervisor at the Sea Terminal in Douglas, so the sea is part of my everyday working life. I enjoy coastal walks, taking in the beautiful scenery and spending time by the sea with my family.

Our community and the ocean

Living on the Isle of Man, the sea connects our community to the world, with ferries bringing passengers, visitors and essential supplies to the island. In my work at the Sea Terminal, I see how much we depend on these connections and how rough weather can disrupt everyday plans. For me, the coast is also a place to relax and enjoy time with family, and it is a special part of what makes the island home.

An issue we see

One issue I notice is litter and plastic washed up along our beaches and coastline. It spoils these beautiful places, and I worry about the harm it can cause to marine wildlife.

What we're doing

Our community could encourage more regular beach cleans and involve local schools, families and businesses. Reducing single-use plastics and providing well-maintained bins near beaches would also help, alongside teaching children why caring for the sea matters

What I wish the world understood

I wish people understood how much a small island community depends on the sea for everyday supplies, work and keeping in touch with family and friends. Protecting it means protecting our home and our children’s future. I would like organisations to support practical local efforts while also tackling pollution before it reaches our shores.

Biggest issues here

Plastic pollution, Water pollution, Sea level rise & coastal erosion

Eritai · Banraeaba

Banraeaba, Kiribati · Plastic & cleanups
Shared by Eritai · 2026-10

About me

Managing Director, I love fishing on the weekends

Our community and the ocean

The ocean is our life, identity, and culture, providing food, transportation, and livelihoods while connecting our families and communities across the islands of Kiribati. It represents our strength and resilience, and we have a responsibility to protect it for future generations, especially from the impacts of climate change.

An issue we see

One major ocean issue in our community is coastal erosion and rising sea levels, which threaten our homes, land and livelihoods. We are witnessing hirelings gradually disappearing and seawater flooding our communities, especially during high tides and storms.

What we're doing

Our community can protect the ocean by reducing plastic waste, organising coastal cleanups, planting mangroves, and promoting sustainable fishing practices. We should also strengthen traditional conservation practices and educate younger generations about the importance of protecting our marine environment.

What I wish the world understood

I wish the world understood that for the people of Kiribati, the ocean is not just our livelihood but an essential part of our identity, culture, and survival. International organization should focus more on practical, community-led solutions that address coastal erosion, marine pollution and climate change while respecting our traditional knowledge.

Biggest issues here

Plastic pollution, Sea level rise & coastal erosion, Lack of government regulation/implementation of sustainable laws
